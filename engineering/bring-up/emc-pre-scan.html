EMC Pre-scan

The EMC pre-scan is an in-house emissions survey of a working prototype. It finds the board's main emission sources, compares them with a shipping product and tests quick fixes, so that the next board revision goes to a formal laboratory with its worst problems already addressed.

What an in-house result can and cannot claim​

The pre-scan uses a tinySA Ultra spectrum analyser. That analyser has no CISPR quasi-peak or average detector, and the antenna used has no calibrated antenna factor. No reading from the pre-scan is therefore a pass or a fail against EN 60945, CISPR 32 or any other emissions standard, and every write-up says so.

What the pre-scan does show is relative:

  • which emitters dominate, and at which frequencies;
  • how the board compares with a shipping, CE-marked marine display of similar class, measured the same way; and
  • whether a fix lowers an emission, and by how much.

Each result is a finding that names a frequency, a source and a candidate fix. Emissions are quantified properly at a formal pre-compliance laboratory, later in the sequence below.

Where the pre-scan sits​

Scadys runs EMC work as a sequence of gates. The pre-scan covers two of them.

GateWorkExit criterion
G0Readiness: schematics, BOM, block diagram and an EMC stress firmware buildAll design inputs complete
G1Power integrity: ripple at every converter output, returns and filter placementNo ripple over 100 mV peak to peak on any rail in the worst-case mode
G2Radiated survey: near-field probes, then an antenna, 30 MHz to 1 GHz, with live fixesTop offenders found, each with at least one workable fix
G3Conducted survey: LISN on the supply, 150 kHz to 30 MHzNo large low-frequency components, and candidate fixes reduce each by at least 3 dB
G4Immunity spot checks: handheld VHF and UHF radios near cables and seamsNo loss of function or reset
G5Enclosure: gasket compression and a pressure-differential bubble testNo sealing defects
G6Formal pre-compliance laboratory: radiated, conducted, ESD and burstFailures quantified, quick fixes re-tested
G7Environmental laboratory: vibration, temperature cycling and salt mistPass or fail, with corrective actions
G8Final verification and technical fileReady for conformity assessment

The pre-scan is gates G2 and G3, run on the bench with the stress firmware from G0.

Instruments​

InstrumentUse
tinySA UltraSpectrum analyser for every scan.
CANBench DuoA passive dual-line DC LISN, giving one 50 Ω port per supply line.
CANBench TrueZA passive common-mode and differential-mode separator, fed from both LISN ports.
Twelve 50 Ω SMA terminatorsOne on every RF port that is not in use.
H-loop and E-stub near-field probesLocating sources on the board and cables.
20 dB preamplifierRaising signals that sit near the analyser's noise floor.
Active antenna, 0.1 GHz to 3 GHz, on a tripodRadiated scans at a fixed distance.
12 V AGM batteryThe supply for every emissions scan, because it adds no conducted noise of its own.

The antenna's stated range starts at 100 MHz, so a radiated reading between 30 MHz and 100 MHz is outside its specification and is reported as indicative only.
